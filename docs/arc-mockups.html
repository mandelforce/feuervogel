Arc Coil Mockups

Four ways the Arc could work once it stops aiming itself. Every screen flies the same route against the same targets. Move the pointer across a screen to fly all four ships yourself.

Super works the same on all four screens: the strands leave their lanes, three bend to the nearest targets, two lash about, and coils jump on from every hit. Damage numbers here are placeholders, not balance.

What the Arc can add

WeaponHow it fires nowWhat the player does
ScatterA fan of darts: 2 straight, then 3, 5, 7; 13 at super.Covers the width of the screen.
LanceOne fast straight bolt that gets wider each level; four flank bolts at super.Lines up and holds.
SeekersA straight laser pair plus homing missiles: 1, 2, 3, 4 with a burst; 10 at super.Lets the missiles find targets.
Arc todayLocks by itself onto the nearest enemy in a narrow cone, 50 to 66% of the screen high. Chain jumps from level 3.Holds fire and stays roughly below something.

Width belongs to Scatter, straight-line damage to the Lance and automatic aim to the Seekers. The Arc today is a second automatic weapon with less reach. Three roles are still free:

  • The weapon you swing (Whip). Its shape depends on how you fly. It rewards movement, where the Lance rewards holding still.
  • The weapon with two modes (Braid and fan). Width while you move, damage while you stop.
  • The weapon that holds on (Latch). You pick the target once and are then free to dodge.

All three share one thing no other weapon has: a continuous beam that hits the moment it touches, with no shots to lead and no gaps to slip through.

How the coils move

LevelStrandsMovement
1OneStraight ahead, as far as the other weapons' shots fly, always drawn. A slow wave runs up it and bright knots travel along it. With nothing to hit, the end forks and flickers.
2TwoSide by side, swaying as mirror images, so the gap between them breathes.
3ThreeThe middle strand sways a quarter-wave out of step with the outer pair, which reads as a braid.
4Four, or three with forked tipsA fourth strand widens the rope. The alternative keeps three and lets each tip make one short jump to a neighbour, a first taste of the super.
SuperFiveThree strands bend to the nearest targets and two lash about in loops. From every hit a coil jumps to the next enemy, up to five times, growing out over a few frames.

From the screenshot I took the thick rope with a hot core, the slow waves, the knots that travel, and the left strand's hook near its end. That hook is the Whip's idle tail. The colour stays the Arc's blue, since orange belongs to enemy bullets.

Reach: 0.65 of the screen height, the middle of what a Scatter or Lance shot flies (0.6 to 0.7). An earlier version of this page had the strands reach the top of the screen, on the wrong belief that the other weapons' shots do.

Recommendation

Build the Whip, with forked tips as level 4. Flown straight it is exactly your brief: lightning to the front, one more strand per level. The moment you move it becomes something the other three weapons are not. It also uses the screenshot's best idea, the strand that kinks and curls.

Rails is the safest but plays like a wider Lance. Braid and fan has the clearest decision, but is dull at level 1. Latch is the most novel, but four leashes at once are hard to read, and it keeps part of the automation you wanted gone.

Forked tips over a fourth strand: four strands are only wider than three, while the fork introduces the jump the super then takes to its extreme.

What building it would touch

  • fireArc, arcTargets, ARC_LV, drawArcs: replaced. The beam becomes a per-frame path with a hit test along it; today it is one bolt every 8 frames.
  • A short history of the ship's x per player, saved in the snapshot, so a resumed run draws the same tail.
  • The Kolibri AI: it stays outside the Arc's cone (ARC_LV cone and slope, arcBand, arcOff, arcMargin) and checks arcLock. With no cone and no lock it needs a new rule, probably "stay off the rope".
  • pbArcCd: the point-blank setting shortens the Arc's cooldown by distance to the locked target. A continuous beam has no cooldown, so it would scale damage by distance to the first hit.
  • tests/superweapon.mjs: one check reads reach, dmg, jumps and bd from ARC_LV[5]; another shoots down a basic fighter with the super Arc.
  • Damage tuning, so boss fights with the Arc stay about as long as with the other weapons. Boss parts take 1.25 times Arc damage today.
  • The Talon drone already fires its own straight shot for the Arc and needs no change. Sounds (chipPlay('arc'), zapSfx) fire per bolt and need a looped or throttled version.
